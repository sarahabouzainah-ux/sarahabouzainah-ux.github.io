About

Designing products
since 2009

I didn’t plan a career in tech. I studied accounting, and then I started learning the field through study and practice. Along the way I found that the skills I already had were a strong fit for it, and in 2009 I began my first role as a web designer. I was soon asked to take on some graphic work too. I said yes, and learned its principles along the way.

From there I moved into UX, which is still the work I do today, across research, interaction design and UI design. Information architecture is part of that craft, and it’s the part I love most: taking a tangled, complex experience and finding the structure that makes it feel obvious. I’m drawn to the hardest version of that problem, enterprise products with dense data, many roles and endless edge cases.

I’ve worked across automotive R&D, telecom, IoT and education, and over time my role grew from designing products to leading the people who design them. I coach designers, set standards, and build the design systems and design operations that let teams deliver consistent quality at scale. I still design interfaces hands-on, and I can adjust the styling of Angular components with HTML, SCSS and some JavaScript, which keeps my designs grounded in what can actually be built.

Over the last few years I’ve woven AI into how I work. It automates the repetitive parts of the process and speeds up discovery, exploration and documentation, which frees my time for the decisions that matter. The result is faster delivery and better quality, because the thinking stays human and the busywork doesn’t.
